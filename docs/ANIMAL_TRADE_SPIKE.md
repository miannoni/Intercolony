# Animal trade technical spike

## 1. Purpose

This is the research-only D1 slice for Feature 6, “Buy and sell animals.” It answers the mandatory API questions and applies the architecture gate in section 10.3: choose the smallest persisted representation that preserves species identity, do not turn the existing goods model into a generic trade-asset framework, and do not write production animal code before the pawn lifecycle is proven (`docs/Playtest corrections plan/Intercolony_Post_0.9.0_Development_Revision_v3.md:1409-1451`).

## 2. The eleven answers

### 1. How vanilla identifies an ordinary trade animal

There are two directions to distinguish. For stock a trader can sell, `StockGenerator_Animals` chooses from loaded `PawnKindDef`s and generates live pawns with `PawnGenerator.GeneratePawn` (`reference/decompiled/RimWorld/StockGenerator_Animals.cs:84-101`). Its filter requires an animal race, permitted wildness, optional tile-temperature suitability, at least one race trade tag matching the generator's `tradeTagsSell`, and race tradeability for which `TraderCanSell()` is true (`reference/decompiled/RimWorld/StockGenerator_Animals.cs:153-184`). `TraderCanSell()` means `Buyable` or `All` (`reference/decompiled/RimWorld/TradeabilityUtility.cs:14-20`; `reference/decompiled/RimWorld/Tradeability.cs:3-8`). The outer trader-stock maker invokes the configured stock generators and rejects generated things whose definitions are not trader-sellable (`reference/decompiled/RimWorld/ThingSetMaker_TraderStock.cs:13-27`). Core Outlander traders demonstrate the concrete `StockGenerator_Animals` configuration and animal sell/buy tags (`reference/vanilla-defs/Core/Defs/TraderKindDefs/TraderKinds_Base_Outlander.xml:248-263`).

Trader-kind willingness is definition-driven. `TraderKindDef.WillTrade` returns true when one of that kind's stock generators handles the `ThingDef`, and a `Tradeable` delegates its willingness check to that method (`reference/decompiled/RimWorld/TraderKindDef.cs:81-90`; `reference/decompiled/RimWorld/Tradeable.cs:100`). For `StockGenerator_Animals`, `HandlesThingDef` requires category `Pawn`, `race.Animal`, non-`None` tradeability, and a matching buy or sell trade tag; `TradeabilityFor` uses `tradeTagsBuy` for animals the trader buys from the player and `tradeTagsSell` for animals the trader sells (`reference/decompiled/RimWorld/StockGenerator_Animals.cs:109-150`).

The live-pawn gate is additional. On a colony map, vanilla's candidate enumerator yields secure prisoners, secure slaves, and player-faction animals, but the animal branch specifically requires `IsAnimal`, no `HostFaction`, no mental state, not downed, and—when requested—acceptable seasonal temperature (`reference/decompiled/RimWorld/TradeUtility.cs:144-167`). The normal current-sellability gate also requires player-sellable tradeability, positive abstract market value, and a supported category, then rejects certain hosted/home-faction pawns and animals currently forming a caravan (`reference/decompiled/RimWorld/TradeUtility.cs:22-40`; `reference/decompiled/RimWorld/TradeUtility.cs:43-73`). Intercolony therefore needs a separate animal candidate path; globally removing its current pawn exclusion would bypass both the trader-kind direction and these live-pawn gates.

### 2. Stable persisted species identity

The race `ThingDef` is the species identity. A `PawnKindDef` contains a `ThingDef race`, so it describes a generation kind layered on a race rather than replacing the race as the species key (`reference/decompiled/Verse/PawnKindDef.cs:9-14`; `reference/decompiled/Verse/PawnKindDef.cs:327`). A generated pawn persists its concrete `Thing.def` and its `kindDef` separately (`reference/decompiled/Verse/Thing.cs:1219-1227`; `reference/decompiled/Verse/Pawn.cs:4563-4573`).

Both references survive ordinary save/load unambiguously while their supplying definitions remain loaded. `Scribe_Defs.Look<T>` writes a definition's `defName` and resolves that name on load (`reference/decompiled/Verse/Scribe_Defs.cs:5-15`). If the content mod is removed, lookup uses `GetNamedSilentFail`, logs the missing definition, and returns null, so neither reference survives removal of its provider (`reference/decompiled/Verse/ScribeExtractor.cs:44-64`).

The persisted promise should therefore carry the race `ThingDef` as the explicit, user-facing species key and the selected `PawnKindDef` wherever a quotation will later generate a pawn. Resolution must require that the saved kind's `race` still equals the saved race. Storing both is not redundant: race answers “which species?”, kind answers “which generator variant of that species?”, and the pair lets load/fulfillment reject a missing or mismatched kind instead of silently choosing another.

### 3. Live-animal market value

Vanilla has pawn-aware value. `StatWorker_MarketValue.GetValueUnfinalized` detects a live `Pawn`, starts from the definition's ordinary unfinalized market value, multiplies by `PriceUtility.PawnQualityPriceFactor`, and adds `PawnQualityPriceOffset` (`reference/decompiled/RimWorld/StatWorker_MarketValue.cs:14-19`). The quality factor accounts for summary health, pawn capacities evaluated for trade price, skills when present, the current life stage's market-value factor, traits, and beauty; the offset adds price impacts from hediffs (`reference/decompiled/RimWorld/PriceUtility.cs:27-88`). The configured generic Market Value stat also has a health contribution, while its generic age stat part is humanlike-only; animal age is represented by the life-stage factor in `PriceUtility` (`reference/vanilla-defs/Core/Defs/Stats/Stats_Basics_General.xml:76-102`; `reference/decompiled/RimWorld/PriceUtility.cs:46`).

Consequently a live pawn's `MarketValue` can differ from the race `ThingDef`'s abstract/base value. Vanilla's player sell and buy prices start from `thing.MarketValue`, not merely the race definition, before applying direction, trader, negotiator, faction, storyteller, and other price factors (`reference/decompiled/RimWorld/TradeUtility.cs:256-282`). A sale quotation based on identified existing animals can therefore use their live pawn values. No training-specific term was found in the verified market-value pipeline; that narrower claim remains unverified rather than assumed.

### 4. Animal representation in a caravan

Animals are caravan-member pawns. A `Caravan` exposes the inner list of its pawn owner through `PawnsListForReading`, and its constructor creates a `ThingOwner<Pawn>` for those members (`reference/decompiled/RimWorld.Planet/Caravan.cs:65`; `reference/decompiled/RimWorld.Planet/Caravan.cs:371-374`). `AddPawnOrItem` sends a `Pawn` to `AddPawn` and all other things to `CaravanInventoryUtility.GiveThing` (`reference/decompiled/RimWorld.Planet/Caravan.cs:509-522`). Caravan inventory is separately enumerated from each member pawn's `inventory.innerContainer` (`reference/decompiled/RimWorld.Planet/CaravanInventoryUtility.cs:18-27`).

Thus a caravan animal must be found in `PawnsListForReading`, not in `AllInventoryItems`. An animal can itself carry inventory, but that inventory does not turn the animal into an inventory item.

### 5. Removing or selling an animal from a caravan

The only complete vanilla sale transaction found starts at `Tradeable_Pawn.ResolveTrade`, which passes each pawn to the active `ITrader`'s `GiveSoldThingToTrader` (`reference/decompiled/RimWorld/Tradeable_Pawn.cs:39-60`). At a settlement, that handler detaches the full single pawn, runs `PreTraded(PlayerSells, ...)`, moves the pawn's inventory to remaining caravan pawns, and puts a non-humanlike pawn into settlement stock (`reference/decompiled/RimWorld.Planet/Settlement_TraderTracker.cs:164-184`). The caravan-trader implementation performs the same sale notification and inventory move, then adds the pawn to the destination caravan (`reference/decompiled/RimWorld.Planet/Caravan_TraderTracker.cs:98-120`). `Pawn.PreTraded` clears guest status and faction, invokes pawn-sold relation handling for flesh pawns, and clears temporary mind state (`reference/decompiled/Verse/Pawn.cs:2599-2643`).

That complete vanilla handler does internally call `SplitOff(1)`. For a pawn's full stack, `Pawn.SplitOff` delegates to `Thing.SplitOff`; the full-count branch despawns/deselects, removes the existing object from its holding owner, and returns that same object rather than creating one (`reference/decompiled/Verse/Pawn.cs:1954-1961`; `reference/decompiled/Verse/Thing.cs:1604-1635`). This is evidence about vanilla's implementation, not a recommendation to route Intercolony pawns through its existing stack abstraction. `Caravan.RemovePawn` by itself merely removes the pawn from the holder and performs none of the transaction, inventory, faction, guest, or relation side effects (`reference/decompiled/RimWorld.Planet/Caravan.cs:530-533`).

I could not find an all-in-one vanilla sale/handoff helper that both avoids the full-stack `SplitOff` call and performs all of those side effects. The later implementation must not call Intercolony's item removal (`SplitOff(...).Destroy(...)`) for a live pawn; it needs a dedicated pawn handoff that preserves the verified `PreTraded`/inventory/holder semantics, with the final destination ownership decision proven separately. That missing all-in-one path is recorded as unverified below.

### 6. Adding a newly acquired animal to a caravan

The safe pawn-holder primitive is `Caravan.AddPawn(pawn, true)`, not inventory insertion. A vanilla quest handoff first sets each pawn to `Faction.OfPlayer` when necessary and then calls `AddPawn(..., addCarriedPawnToWorldPawnsIfAny: true)` (`reference/decompiled/RimWorld/QuestPart_GiveToCaravan.cs:84-95`). `Caravan.AddPawn` rejects null/dead pawns, handles a carried pawn, despawns/deselects the pawn, and adds it to the caravan's pawn owner (`reference/decompiled/RimWorld.Planet/Caravan.cs:466-506`). The boolean also covers a carried pawn's world-pawn bookkeeping.

Vanilla settlement trade purchase does the same essential ownership-and-holder sequence after its stock detachment: `PreTraded(PlayerBuys, ...)` followed by `caravan.AddPawn(pawn, true)` (`reference/decompiled/RimWorld.Planet/Settlement_TraderTracker.cs:187-207`). `PreTraded(PlayerBuys, ...)` clears guest status and calls `SetFaction(Faction.OfPlayer)` outside the slave-specific branch (`reference/decompiled/Verse/Pawn.cs:2615-2626`). Intercolony pickup should therefore create or obtain a live pawn, transfer it to the player faction, and add that pawn to the caravan; it must not use `CaravanInventoryUtility.GiveThing` or a carrier's item container.

### 7. Creating and spawning a purchased animal at the colony

The verified creation API is `PawnGenerator.GeneratePawn`, whose public overload accepts a `PawnKindDef`, optional faction, and optional tile (`reference/decompiled/Verse/PawnGenerator.cs:121-136`). A directly analogous vanilla animal-arrival path obtains an animal-capable map entry cell, chooses a nearby walkable cell, generates a non-dead/non-downed pawn from the selected kind, spawns the resulting pawn with `GenSpawn.Spawn`, and then transfers it to `Faction.OfPlayer` (`reference/decompiled/RimWorld/IncidentWorker_FarmAnimalsWanderIn.cs:47-79`). `GenSpawn.Spawn(Thing, IntVec3, Map, ...)` is the spawn operation for the already-generated pawn (`reference/decompiled/Verse/GenSpawn.cs:26-28`; `reference/decompiled/Verse/GenSpawn.cs:86-113`).

Do not replace pawn generation with `ThingMaker.MakeThing`. `PawnGenerator` internally creates the race object and then immediately assigns the requested `kindDef`, faction, and pawn components before continuing generation (`reference/decompiled/Verse/PawnGenerator.cs:726-735`). Passing `Faction.OfPlayer` to generation also completes Tameness training for an animal (`reference/decompiled/Verse/PawnGenerator.cs:828-836`). Calling `ThingMaker.MakeThing` directly would omit that pawn-generation pipeline and would not lock the promised `PawnKindDef`.

The proven minimum is therefore: validate that the saved kind still belongs to the saved non-humanlike animal race; call `PawnGenerator.GeneratePawn`; find a pawn-safe cell; spawn the returned `Pawn`; and ensure its faction is the player faction. The farm-animal incident proves an edge-arrival placement. I did not find a dedicated “purchased animal delivery” helper or a vanilla guarantee corresponding exactly to the plan's phrase “healthy ordinary animal.”

### 8. Faction and ownership transfer

Vanilla has no single before-sale faction for all trader stock. `StockGenerator_Animals` initially generates sale animals with a null faction (`reference/decompiled/RimWorld/StockGenerator_Animals.cs:93-101`); when those wares are used by a visiting trader caravan, `PawnGroupKindWorker_Trader` changes pawn wares to the trader caravan's faction (`reference/decompiled/RimWorld/PawnGroupKindWorker_Trader.cs:112-123`). On purchase, `Pawn.PreTraded(PlayerBuys, ...)` performs the ownership transfer by calling `SetFaction(Faction.OfPlayer)` (`reference/decompiled/Verse/Pawn.cs:2615-2626`). `Pawn.SetFaction` performs the faction-change bookkeeping and completes tameness when the destination is the player faction and the pawn is an animal (`reference/decompiled/Verse/Pawn.cs:2694-2780`, especially `:2760-2768`).

For an Intercolony purchase generated only at fulfillment, the animal should be in `Faction.OfPlayer` before it is finally spawned or added to the player's caravan; the verified generation and handoff paths above support either generating with that faction or calling `SetFaction(Faction.OfPlayer)` before `AddPawn` (`reference/decompiled/Verse/PawnGenerator.cs:121-136`; `reference/decompiled/RimWorld/QuestPart_GiveToCaravan.cs:84-95`).

Sale is destination-specific. `Pawn.PreTraded(PlayerSells, ...)` first clears a non-null faction (`reference/decompiled/Verse/Pawn.cs:2627-2639`). A visiting pawn trader then explicitly assigns the trader's faction (`reference/decompiled/RimWorld/Pawn_TraderTracker.cs:205-232`), while a world settlement leaves a non-humanlike pawn factionless in stock (`reference/decompiled/RimWorld.Planet/Settlement_TraderTracker.cs:164-184`). Therefore the spike cannot claim that every sold animal should end in the buyer faction. The ownership transfer side effect is verified; the correct long-term holder/faction for Intercolony's abstract settlement handoff is not.

### 9. Temporary, quest, lodger, employee, and ownership exclusions

The animal-sale predicate must be stricter than “is a pawn in the caravan.” At minimum it should require alive/not destroyed, `RaceProps.Animal`, explicitly not `RaceProps.Humanlike`, `Faction == Faction.OfPlayer`, `HomeFaction == Faction.OfPlayer`, `HostFaction == null`, not downed, not in a mental state, not a prisoner, not a slave, not a colonist, not a quest lodger/helper, and not an active Intercolony employee. The vanilla map-trade animal gate supplies the animal/player-faction/no-host/downed/mental subset (`reference/decompiled/RimWorld/TradeUtility.cs:144-167`). `HomeFaction` returns an extra home or mini-faction ahead of the base player faction, so checking it protects against apparent player-faction pawns whose real home is elsewhere (`reference/decompiled/Verse/Pawn.cs:1064-1080`). `IsQuestLodger` is true for pawns with an extra home or mini-faction, and `IsQuestHelper` is a narrower lodger state (`reference/decompiled/RimWorld/QuestUtility.cs:524-550`).

This deliberately exceeds vanilla's trader-relative exclusions. `PlayerSellableNow` rejects an extra host only when it equals the current trader faction, a quest lodger only when its extra home equals that trader, and a non-slave/non-prisoner pawn only when its home equals that trader (`reference/decompiled/RimWorld/TradeUtility.cs:62-72`). Intercolony's invariant is stronger: it must never sell any pawn the colony does not own, regardless of which counterparty happens to be receiving it.

The employee cross-check is decisive even though the humanlike gate should already reject every current employee. Active employees are intentionally quest lodgers carrying their employer as an extra home faction (`Source/Intercolony/Labor/EmploymentContract.cs:69-100`; `Source/Intercolony/Labor/EmploymentService.cs:1139-1168`). The mod's authoritative employee test is an active `EmploymentContract` whose pawn reference matches (`Source/Intercolony/Labor/EmploymentService.cs:1107-1129`). `docs/LABOR_TECHNICAL_NOTES.md` records that vanilla excludes lodgers from ordinary caravan formation and that Intercolony's labor-only patch re-admits only its employees while retaining vanilla's other gates (`docs/LABOR_TECHNICAL_NOTES.md:165-185`); the underlying vanilla predicate confirms the lodger/helper, prisoner-security, downed, mental-state, faction, and lord checks (`reference/decompiled/RimWorld.Planet/CaravanFormingUtility.cs:264-276`). Animal-sale eligibility must therefore check both `!pawn.IsQuestLodger()` and `!EmploymentService.IsEmployee(pawn)` as defense in depth. Humans, prisoners, colonists, slaves, employees, and converted former employees remain permanently out of scope because the explicit non-humanlike-animal gate runs before any species or order match.

### 10. Bonds and relations on voluntary sale

Normal vanilla pawn sale reaches `Pawn.PreTraded(PlayerSells, ...)` through `Tradeable_Pawn.ResolveTrade` and an `ITrader` handler (`reference/decompiled/RimWorld/Tradeable_Pawn.cs:39-60`; `reference/decompiled/RimWorld/Pawn_TraderTracker.cs:149-160`). For a flesh pawn, `PreTraded` calls `relations.Notify_PawnSold(playerNegotiator)` (`reference/decompiled/Verse/Pawn.cs:2627-2639`).

`Notify_PawnSold` examines related pawns with mood, finds the most important relation to the sold pawn, removes a direct `Bond`, and applies that relation's configured `soldThought` memories (`reference/decompiled/RimWorld/Pawn_RelationsTracker.cs:940-963`). Core's `Bond` relation configures `SoldMyBondedAnimal` and `SoldMyBondedAnimalMood` (`reference/vanilla-defs/Core/Defs/PawnRelationDefs/PawnRelations_Misc.xml:9-20`). The first is a 60-day social memory with -10 opinion and the second a 60-day -10 mood memory (`reference/vanilla-defs/Core/Defs/ThoughtDefs/Thoughts_Memory_Social.xml:300-330`). Accordingly a normal voluntary sale removes the bond and applies both configured effects when Bond is the most important relation for an eligible related pawn. A custom Intercolony handoff that merely removes or destroys the pawn would skip this behavior.

### 11. Multiple `PawnKindDef`s per species

Yes, the engine permits multiple `PawnKindDef`s to point to one race. Debug spawning explicitly enumerates every kind whose `race == pawn.def` (`reference/decompiled/Verse/DebugToolsSpawning.cs:534-548`). `RaceProperties.AnyPawnKind` scans the definition database and returns the first kind whose race properties match, rather than enforcing or documenting uniqueness (`reference/decompiled/Verse/RaceProperties.cs:394-412`). Vanilla humanlike definitions provide concrete many-kinds-to-one-race examples—Mechanitor and Sanguophage Thrall both use race `Human`, as does Core Pirate—but humanlikes are outside this feature (`reference/vanilla-defs/Biotech/Defs/PawnKindDefs_Humanlikes/PawnKinds_Special.xml:5-8`; `reference/vanilla-defs/Biotech/Defs/PawnKindDefs_Humanlikes/PawnKinds_Special.xml:174-176`; `reference/vanilla-defs/Core/Defs/PawnKindDefs_Humanlikes/PawnKinds_Pirate.xml:111-113`).

A quotation that will later generate an animal must lock both race `ThingDef` and the exact `PawnKindDef`, then require `kind.race == race` before fulfillment. Race alone preserves species but leaves generation-variant selection ambiguous; recording both makes the promised species explicit and lets fulfillment detect a missing or mismatched kind rather than silently selecting another. I found no concrete duplicate non-humanlike animal kind in the installed vanilla XML, so content-mod animal duplicates are a compatibility risk permitted by the engine, not a behavior demonstrated by these vanilla defs.

## 3. Representation comparison

### Current code constraints

The actual current save schema is 24, not merely the number stated in the planning prompt. `IntercolonyWorldComponent.CurrentSaveVersion` is 24, its governing comment requires a version bump and migration whenever saved shape changes, and it deep-saves sales orders, requests, and purchase orders as separate collections (`Source/Intercolony/Core/IntercolonyWorldComponent.cs:25-30`; `Source/Intercolony/Core/IntercolonyWorldComponent.cs:671-690`).

The current sales shape is one `SalesOrder` containing one deep-saved `OrderLine`; that line persists `ThingDef thingDef`, quantity, optional quality, allowed stuff, and condition floor (`Source/Intercolony/Orders/SalesOrder.cs:55-100`; `Source/Intercolony/Orders/SalesOrder.cs:142-193`; `Source/Intercolony/Orders/OrderLine.cs:20-64`). Procurement separately persists the requested `ThingDef`/stuff/quantity and deep quotation list, then copies the accepted promise into a `PurchaseOrder` with `ThingDef`, stuff, quality, quantity, price, timing, fulfillment, and status (`Source/Intercolony/Procurement/PurchaseRequest.cs:41-110`; `Source/Intercolony/Procurement/PurchaseRequest.cs:121-146`; `Source/Intercolony/Procurement/PurchaseRequest.cs:215-250`; `Source/Intercolony/Procurement/PurchaseOrder.cs:44-80`; `Source/Intercolony/Procurement/PurchaseOrder.cs:116-145`).

Those shapes are item-oriented in behavior, not just naming. Caravan validation scans inventory items and counts stack units, while colony validation and taking operate on stored things and destroy whole/minified or split stacks (`Source/Intercolony/Orders/OrderValidation.cs:146-205`; `Source/Intercolony/Orders/OrderValidation.cs:212-332`). Sales delivery again scans `AllInventoryItems`, splits matching stacks, and destroys the handed-over pieces (`Source/Intercolony/Orders/SalesOrderService.cs:190-243`; `Source/Intercolony/Orders/SalesOrderService.cs:457-490`). Procurement makes goods with `ThingMaker.MakeThing`, stack limits, stuff and quality, places them as things at the colony, or inserts them into a caravan pawn's item inventory (`Source/Intercolony/Procurement/PurchaseOrderService.cs:177-252`; `Source/Intercolony/Procurement/PurchaseOrderService.cs:314-366`). None of those item handoff paths is safe for a live pawn.

The current classifier boundary is explicit and must remain: `PassesIntrinsicRules` rejects `def.category == ThingCategory.Pawn` at `Source/Intercolony/Market/IntercolonyProductClassifier.cs:159-163`. `IsFungibleTradeItem` independently accepts only items or minifiable buildings (`Source/Intercolony/Market/IntercolonyProductClassifier.cs:206-219`). Animal discovery must be a separate mode/path, not a removal of either guard.

### Option A — existing entities, race in existing definition fields

**Persisted state and schema.** The existing `thingDef` fields could carry the race `ThingDef`, and quantities could mean head count. That adds no field and therefore forces no save-schema change: schema remains 24. Old goods orders are unchanged.

**Special cases.** This option needs animal detection and special handling at at least seven verified item-shaped sites: caravan validation, caravan removal, colony validation, colony taking, procurement creation, colony placement, and caravan collection (`Source/Intercolony/Orders/OrderValidation.cs:146-332`; `Source/Intercolony/Orders/SalesOrderService.cs:457-490`; `Source/Intercolony/Procurement/PurchaseOrderService.cs:177-252`; `Source/Intercolony/Procurement/PurchaseOrderService.cs:314-366`). Because there is no discriminator, every route must infer “animal” from the category of `thingDef`. Seller-side buyer pickup must also be rejected or separately implemented; the D1/D3 plan only proves physical seller delivery.

**Failure mode.** Option A cannot persist the selected `PawnKindDef`, so a purchase quotation cannot lock the generation variant. Calling `AnyPawnKind` later would select the first matching kind, not a canonical one (`reference/decompiled/Verse/RaceProperties.cs:394-412`). This preserves species identity but fails the required exact generation-variant lock for procurement quotations. If the species mod is removed, the current `Scribe_Defs` load resolves `thingDef` to null; current validity checks then remove the sales order, RFQ, or purchase order, logging the loss—and for a paid purchase explicitly warning that the silver is gone (`Source/Intercolony/Core/IntercolonyWorldComponent.cs:718-768`).

### Option B — existing entities with an explicit goods/animal discriminator

**Persisted state and schema.** The smallest safe form of B adds an explicit subject discriminator at each independently saved order stage, retains the existing `thingDef` as race for animals, and adds the exact `PawnKindDef` to every persisted quotation/order stage that can later generate or validate that promise. At minimum this affects `OrderLine`, `PurchaseRequest`, `Quotation`, and `PurchaseOrder`. It is a real saved-shape change, so from the verified current schema 24 it requires the next sequential schema (currently 25) and a migration/default making every old record “goods” (`Source/Intercolony/Core/IntercolonyWorldComponent.cs:25-30`; current serialization sites above). Implementation must re-check the current version rather than hard-code 25 if the repository advances first.

**Special cases.** B still needs the same seven animal branches/gates in the existing validation and handoff sites. The discriminator makes those branches explicit and gives old goods a safe default; it does not make item mechanics polymorphic or safe for pawns. The statuses, settlement identity, deadlines, quantity/head count, unit price, payment, cancellation, and supplier-delivery-versus-pickup lifecycle remain coherent without duplication, but animal handlers must be narrow dedicated branches which never fall through to stack, stuff, quality, `ThingMaker`, item inventory, or destruction logic (`Source/Intercolony/Orders/SalesOrder.cs:55-122`; `Source/Intercolony/Procurement/PurchaseOrder.cs:44-96`).

**Failure mode.** If either saved race or saved kind disappears, or if the surviving kind no longer references the saved race, the animal promise is unresolved and must not fall back to another kind. Current null-def cleanup is not sufficient for a paid purchase because it drops the obligation and loses paid silver (`Source/Intercolony/Core/IntercolonyWorldComponent.cs:753-768`). B therefore also forces explicit post-load validity and a visible unresolved-obligation policy for animal purchases.

### Option C — narrow parallel animal-specific entities

**Persisted state and schema.** C needs parallel persisted animal sale, animal request/quotation, and animal purchase records carrying race, kind where generation is promised, head count, counterparty, price, timing, fulfillment, status, and outcome. It also needs new world-component collections and serialization. From current schema 24 that forces the next sequential version and migration initializing the new collections (`Source/Intercolony/Core/IntercolonyWorldComponent.cs:25-30`; `Source/Intercolony/Core/IntercolonyWorldComponent.cs:671-690`). This is materially more new state than B even if the new records reuse existing status enums.

**Special cases.** C introduces zero animal branches inside the seven existing goods validation/delivery sites because the live-pawn paths are parallel. The cost is parallel lifecycle services and UI/world-component dispatch for sales, RFQs/quotes, purchases, advancement, cancellation, load validation, and history. That is isolation, but it duplicates mechanisms whose non-item state transitions already fit head-count orders.

**Failure mode.** C needs its own equivalent of every current `IsValidAfterLoad` and component cleanup rule. Missing race or kind must leave a visible unresolved obligation rather than silently selecting a different kind; an accepted paid order in particular must not be discarded. Without those explicit rules it fails in the same way as B, only in a second set of entity types.

## 4. Recommendation

Recommend **B: reuse the existing order lifecycles, add a narrow explicit subject discriminator, and persist race plus exact generation kind where required**.

Option A is the only no-schema-change choice, but it does not preserve a procurement quotation's `PawnKindDef` identity and would rely on implicit category checks throughout item-shaped code. It therefore fails section 10.3's identity gate. Option C gives the cleanest mechanical isolation, but duplicates multiple persisted lifecycles and world collections even though the existing non-item state—counterparty, head count, unit price, timing, payment, status, cancellation, and delivery mode—still applies. B is the smallest persisted shape that meets both halves of the identity rule: race is the species, kind is the exact generation variant.

This recommendation does **not** propose a generic trade-asset abstraction. It is one discriminator and animal-only fields/branches for the second concrete use case. The existing `ThingCategory.Pawn` and fungibility exclusions remain intact. The later D2/D3 implementation should branch before any item validator, maker, inventory, stack, or destruction code; prohibit animal buyer-pickup sales unless separately proven; and complete payment only after the dedicated live-pawn handoff succeeds.

## 5. Open risks

1. **Humanlike admission is the highest-severity boundary risk.** Several vanilla interfaces traffic in `Pawn`, not “animal.” `Caravan.AddPawn` can auto-capture a pawn, and `CaravanUtility.ShouldAutoCapture` is explicitly a humanlike-pawn path (`reference/decompiled/RimWorld.Planet/Caravan.cs:466-506`; `reference/decompiled/RimWorld.Planet/CaravanUtility.cs:73-84`). Every entry point—listing, quote creation, save-load validation, generation, caravan scan, and handoff—must begin with the explicit `RaceProps.Animal && !RaceProps.Humanlike` gate before matching race or kind. Colonists, prisoners, slaves, employees, quest lodgers, and all other humanlikes are permanently out of scope.

2. **There is no verified no-split all-in-one caravan sale API.** Vanilla's complete settlement transaction has required pawn side effects but internally detaches with a full-count `SplitOff` (`reference/decompiled/RimWorld.Planet/Settlement_TraderTracker.cs:164-184`). Intercolony's existing removal destroys split item stacks (`Source/Intercolony/Orders/SalesOrderService.cs:457-490`). D3 must implement and test a pawn-only holder handoff; it must not reuse the current removal helper or treat vanilla's full-stack implementation as permission to generalize stack operations to pawns.

3. **The abstract buyer's final pawn lifetime is unresolved.** Visiting traders and world settlements assign different post-sale factions/holders (`reference/decompiled/RimWorld/Pawn_TraderTracker.cs:205-232`; `reference/decompiled/RimWorld.Planet/Settlement_TraderTracker.cs:164-184`). Choosing faction, world-pawn retention, or disposal for an Intercolony settlement is part of D3 proof, not something this spike can infer.

4. **Paid procurement cannot inherit current removed-content loss unchanged.** Current post-load cleanup can drop an unresolved paid purchase and explicitly says the silver is gone (`Source/Intercolony/Core/IntercolonyWorldComponent.cs:753-768`). Animal orders add a second required def and need a recoverable unresolved state or another owner-approved policy.

5. **Pawn-aware quotation timing is unresolved.** Existing animals have a verified live `MarketValue`, but a future ungenerated procurement promise has no age, health, traits, or hediffs yet (`reference/decompiled/RimWorld/StatWorker_MarketValue.cs:14-19`; `reference/decompiled/RimWorld/PriceUtility.cs:27-88`). D2 must choose and disclose a baseline or generate the actual pawn at a defined point without deep-saving speculative pawns merely to quote them.

6. **Bond effects depend on the transaction notification.** Skipping `Pawn.PreTraded(PlayerSells, ...)` also skips `Notify_PawnSold`, bond removal, and the configured thoughts (`reference/decompiled/Verse/Pawn.cs:2627-2639`; `reference/decompiled/RimWorld/Pawn_RelationsTracker.cs:940-963`). A dedicated D3 handoff needs a regression test for this side effect.

## 7. Follow-up addendum — specification-based animal trade (2026-08-09)

This addendum addresses the stronger product rule that an animal order promises a specification—species, sex, life stage, and optionally pregnancy—rather than identified individual pawns. It adds to the D1 conclusions above: the saved race and generation kind remain necessary, but they are no longer the complete promise.

### Pregnancy on the buying side

**Verdict: safe initialization is source-verified for a freshly generated, non-egg-layer vanilla live-bearing animal; it is not a universal promise for every animal race.** The ordinary mating job ends by calling `PawnUtility.Mated(male, female)` (`reference/decompiled/RimWorld/JobDriver_Mate.cs:41-44`). For a non-egg-layer female, that method's successful-pregnancy branch constructs `HediffDefOf.Pregnant`, calls `SetParents(null, male, null)`, and adds the hediff to the female; the 50-percent roll belongs to natural conception, not to hediff initialization (`reference/decompiled/RimWorld/PawnUtility.cs:360-374`). `PawnGenerationRequest.AllowPregnant` is not an animal solution: the generator's pregnancy branch constructs `HediffDefOf.PregnantHuman`, not `HediffDefOf.Pregnant` (`reference/decompiled/Verse/PawnGenerator.cs:1217-1235`).

The animal hediff has no hidden due date. `Hediff_Pregnant.GestationProgress` is exactly the base hediff `Severity`; ticking advances it by food-dependent body-resource growth divided by `RaceProps.gestationPeriodDays`, and progress at or above 1.0 causes animal birth and removal of the hediff (`reference/decompiled/Verse/Hediff_Pregnant.cs:22-32`; `reference/decompiled/Verse/Hediff_Pregnant.cs:84-139`). The definition supplies initial severity `0.001`, three severity stages, and the pregnancy message component (`reference/vanilla-defs/Core/Defs/HediffDefs/Hediffs_Global_Misc.xml:573-617`). `Hediff.PostMake` applies that initial severity, `Pawn_HealthTracker.AddHediff(HediffDef, ...)` makes and installs the hediff, and `Hediff_Pregnant.PostAdd` initializes its cached stage (`reference/decompiled/Verse/Hediff.cs:546-549`; `reference/decompiled/Verse/Pawn_HealthTracker.cs:210-236`; `reference/decompiled/Verse/Hediff_Pregnant.cs:78-82`).

The persisted pregnancy state is likewise explicit. Base `Hediff` serialization saves `ageTicks` and `severity`; `HediffWithParents` saves optional father and mother references plus an optional gene set; and `Hediff_Pregnant` saves `lastStage` (`reference/decompiled/Verse/Hediff.cs:390-409`; `reference/decompiled/Verse/HediffWithParents.cs:84-94`; `reference/decompiled/Verse/Hediff_Pregnant.cs:228-232`). A father is not required for an animal pregnancy to run or give birth: the vanilla debug hediff path adds a hediff and invokes `PostDebugAdd`, while `Hediff_Pregnant.PostDebugAdd` initializes parents only for a humanlike when Biotech is active and leaves animal parents null; animal birth adds the father relation only when the father reference is non-null (`reference/decompiled/Verse/DebugTools_Health.cs:134-151`; `reference/decompiled/Verse/Hediff_Pregnant.cs:157-185`; `reference/decompiled/Verse/Hediff_Pregnant.cs:247-252`). If Intercolony does keep a real father reference, `SetParents` registers it for world-pawn preservation and the references are removed when the hediff is removed (`reference/decompiled/Verse/HediffWithParents.cs:18-31`; `reference/decompiled/RimWorld.Planet/WorldPawns.cs:504-529`). A parentless animal pregnancy avoids creating or retaining a throwaway father and follows the parent-null behavior that the local animal code already tolerates.

That safe verdict is deliberately narrower than “every animal can be pregnant.” `PawnUtility.Mated` sends females with `CompEggLayer` to `Fertilize(male)` instead of adding `HediffDefOf.Pregnant`; only the non-egg-layer branch creates the pregnancy hediff (`reference/decompiled/RimWorld/PawnUtility.cs:360-374`). `RaceProperties.gestationPeriodDays` defaults to `-1`, and configuration validation reports normal-flesh races which do not configure it (`reference/decompiled/Verse/RaceProperties.cs:166`; `reference/decompiled/Verse/RaceProperties.cs:537-540`). A buy-side pregnancy option therefore needs a capability gate at least as strict as the verified live-bearing path; directly adding `Pregnant` to an egg layer or an unconfigured mod race is not justified by these sources.

Pregnancy does **not** change vanilla's computed trade value. Pawn market value uses `PriceUtility.PawnQualityPriceFactor` and `PawnQualityPriceOffset` (`reference/decompiled/RimWorld/StatWorker_MarketValue.cs:14-19`). Capacity calculation for trade price ignores every hediff whose `priceImpact` is false, and the offset method likewise considers only `priceImpact` or a non-zero `priceOffset` (`reference/decompiled/Verse/PawnCapacityUtility.cs:74-96`; `reference/decompiled/RimWorld/PriceUtility.cs:67-87`). The animal `Pregnant` definition declares neither field, whose code defaults are false and zero; consequently even its middle- and late-stage Moving penalties are intentionally ignored by the trade-price capacity calculation (`reference/decompiled/Verse/HediffDef.cs:68-70`; `reference/vanilla-defs/Core/Defs/HediffDefs/Hediffs_Global_Misc.xml:573-617`). Intercolony must supply its own pregnancy premium or discount if pregnancy is to be separately priced on either side.

### Sex and life stage as promises

For a newly generated pawn, `FixedGender` is a reliable sex request: `PawnGenerator` assigns it before consulting `PawnKindDef.fixedGender`, `RaceProperties.hasGenders`, or `RaceProperties.forceGender`, and its world-pawn candidate validator rejects a redressed pawn whose gender does not match the request (`reference/decompiled/Verse/PawnGenerator.cs:741-766`; `reference/decompiled/Verse/PawnGenerator.cs:547-562`). The installed vanilla animal base explicitly has genders; the only local vanilla `hasGenders=false` override found is the mechanoid race base, not an animal (`reference/vanilla-defs/Core/Defs/ThingDefs_Races/Races_Animal_Base.xml:49-68`; `reference/vanilla-defs/Core/Defs/ThingDefs_Races/Races_Mechanoid.xml:20-30`). No vanilla animal `forceGender` or `PawnKindDef.fixedGender` restriction was found in the installed definitions. Modded single-sex or genderless animals remain a compatibility case and should be rejected if the generated pawn's `gender` does not equal the promised value, rather than assuming their definitions follow Core.

`AllowedDevelopmentalStages = Adult` does not mean “mature animal.” The generator mechanically tests the requested flag against `LifeStageUtility.CalculateDevelopmentalStage` when choosing an age (`reference/decompiled/Verse/PawnGenerator.cs:1403-1425`; `reference/decompiled/Verse/LifeStageUtility.cs:54-68`). However, `LifeStageDef.developmentalStage` defaults to `Adult`, and Core's `AnimalBaby`, `AnimalBabyTiny`, and `AnimalJuvenile` definitions do not override that field (`reference/decompiled/RimWorld/LifeStageDef.cs:23-29`; `reference/vanilla-defs/Core/Defs/Misc/LifeStageDefs/LifeStages.xml:176-221`). The flag is therefore consulted but cannot distinguish Core animal babies, juveniles, and adults.

For a non-humanlike pawn, vanilla defines the adult threshold as the `minAge` of the last entry in `race.lifeStageAges`, and `Pawn_AgeTracker.Adult` compares biological age ticks with that threshold (`reference/decompiled/Verse/Pawn_AgeTracker.cs:206-230`). The correct fulfilment request is consequently a `FixedBiologicalAge` at or above that race-specific last-stage threshold; the generator writes the requested biological age directly into `AgeBiologicalTicks` (`reference/decompiled/Verse/PawnGenerator.cs:1314-1325`). Alpaca demonstrates why this must be race-derived: its baby, juvenile, and adult stages begin at 0, 0.2, and 0.3333 years respectively (`reference/vanilla-defs/Core/Defs/ThingDefs_Races/Races_Animal_SheepGroup.xml:241-258`).

On the selling side, an “adult female alpaca” match should read the live pawn's species from `pawn.def`, require `pawn.gender == Gender.Female`, and require `pawn.ageTracker.Adult`; those exact members are the persisted `ThingDef`, pawn gender field, and vanilla adult predicate (`reference/decompiled/Verse/Thing.cs:15-18`; `reference/decompiled/Verse/Pawn.cs:14-24`; `reference/decompiled/Verse/Pawn_AgeTracker.cs:210-230`). `pawn.DevelopmentalStage` is not a substitute for the last check because it simply returns the current `LifeStageDef.developmentalStage`, which has the animal-default problem above (`reference/decompiled/Verse/Pawn.cs:764`; `reference/decompiled/RimWorld/LifeStageDef.cs:23-29`). Pregnancy matching, when requested, is a separate `HasHediff(HediffDefOf.Pregnant)` test, not an implication of female sex or adulthood (`reference/decompiled/Verse/HediffSet.cs:619-631`; `reference/vanilla-defs/Core/Defs/HediffDefs/Hediffs_Global_Misc.xml:573-583`).

### Pricing a specification before a pawn exists

Of the four promised attributes, vanilla directly prices two. Species supplies the race definition's abstract/base Market Value; for example, Alpaca defines 350 silver (`reference/decompiled/Verse/ThingDef.cs:480-485`; `reference/vanilla-defs/Core/Defs/ThingDefs_Races/Races_Animal_SheepGroup.xml:208-220`). Life stage multiplies the pawn-quality factor by `pawn.ageTracker.CurLifeStage.marketValueFactor` (`reference/decompiled/RimWorld/PriceUtility.cs:27-64`). Core's generic animal stages set baby to 0.4 and juvenile to 0.75, while adult inherits the code default of 1.0, so otherwise equal Core animals span 0.4–1.0 from baby to adult: adult is 2.5 times the baby factor and four-thirds of the juvenile factor (`reference/vanilla-defs/Core/Defs/Misc/LifeStageDefs/LifeStages.xml:176-221`; `reference/decompiled/RimWorld/LifeStageDef.cs:70-78`). Content-defined life stages can supply a different `marketValueFactor`, so the selected race's actual last/current stage definition remains authoritative (`reference/decompiled/RimWorld/LifeStageDef.cs:66-78`; `reference/decompiled/Verse/Pawn_AgeTracker.cs:189-203`).

Sex has no branch in the pawn market-value factor or offset, and pregnancy has no effective price hook as established above (`reference/decompiled/RimWorld/PriceUtility.cs:27-88`; `reference/decompiled/Verse/PawnCapacityUtility.cs:89-123`). Therefore vanilla values an otherwise identical adult male and adult female equally and adds no value for unborn offspring. The requested experience—where sex and pregnancy are separately priced—requires explicit Intercolony modifiers on both buy and sell quotations; simply reading a selling pawn's `MarketValue` will not produce those distinctions.

A definition-only representative quote is possible without generating a pawn, but it is an Intercolony baseline rather than the exact vanilla pawn-aware value: read the race's abstract Market Value through `GetStatValueAbstract`/`BaseMarketValue`, multiply by the selected life stage's factor, then apply the mod's explicit sex and pregnancy policy (`reference/decompiled/RimWorld/StatExtension.cs:17-20`; `reference/decompiled/Verse/ThingDef.cs:480-485`; `reference/decompiled/RimWorld/LifeStageDef.cs:66-78`). It omits the live inputs which `PawnQualityPriceFactor` and `PawnQualityPriceOffset` read—summary health, capacities, optional skills, traits, beauty, and price-impacting hediffs—and therefore cannot claim to equal a particular future pawn's eventual `MarketValue` (`reference/decompiled/RimWorld/PriceUtility.cs:27-88`).

There is no verified vanilla market-value API taking a race/sex/stage/pregnancy specification; the pawn-quality methods require an actual `Pawn`, and `StatWorker_MarketValue` enters that path only when its request contains a pawn (`reference/decompiled/RimWorld/PriceUtility.cs:27`; `reference/decompiled/RimWorld/PriceUtility.cs:67`; `reference/decompiled/RimWorld/StatWorker_MarketValue.cs:14-19`). Generating a probe is not a zero-cost abstract calculation: it makes a pawn and components, generates initial hediffs, notifies the scenario, and can notify a non-null faction that the pawn joined (`reference/decompiled/Verse/PawnGenerator.cs:726-735`; `reference/decompiled/Verse/PawnGenerator.cs:912-953`; `reference/decompiled/Verse/PawnGenerator.cs:1048-1053`). A non-newborn probe must set `ForceGenerateNewPawn`; otherwise generation may redress and remove an existing free world pawn (`reference/decompiled/Verse/PawnGenerator.cs:199-224`). It must also be explicitly discarded after measurement, as vanilla's own generated-pawn debug probes do with `Find.WorldPawns.PassToWorld(..., PawnDiscardDecideMode.Discard)` (`reference/decompiled/Verse/DamageWorker_Blunt.cs:80-100`; `reference/decompiled/RimWorld.Planet/WorldPawns.cs:200-233`). Even with those precautions, local source does not establish a side-effect-free quote-generation configuration, so the definition-based baseline is the safer V1 quotation policy unless exact live-pawn pricing is a product requirement.

### Sex and life stage at the eligibility gate

Nothing in the D1 exclusion set conflicts with applying sex and life-stage filters after ownership and safety eligibility. Vanilla's colony animal enumerator checks player faction, animal identity, host faction, mental state, downed state, and optionally temperature, but not gender or age (`reference/decompiled/RimWorld/TradeUtility.cs:144-167`). `EverPlayerSellable` is a definition-level tradeability, positive-value, and category gate, while the pawn-specific part of `PlayerSellableNow` checks trader-relative home/host/lodger state and caravan formation, not gender or age (`reference/decompiled/RimWorld/TradeUtility.cs:22-40`; `reference/decompiled/RimWorld/TradeUtility.cs:43-73`). `StockGenerator_Animals` likewise gates animal definitions and kinds by tradeability, tags, wildness, and temperature without a sex or life-stage clause (`reference/decompiled/RimWorld/StockGenerator_Animals.cs:109-184`). A juvenile player-owned animal which passes the existing D1 safety and ownership predicate is therefore not excluded from sale merely for being juvenile; “sell me your young alpacas” remains possible.

### Scope consequence

Specification trade is materially more work than adding one discriminator plus one passive specification object. The terms must survive independently through `OrderLine`, `PurchaseRequest`, each `Quotation`, and the accepted `PurchaseOrder`, all of which serialize their own current promise fields; purchase acceptance copies request and quotation state into a new order (`Source/Intercolony/Orders/OrderLine.cs:20-64`; `Source/Intercolony/Procurement/PurchaseRequest.cs:41-110`; `Source/Intercolony/Procurement/PurchaseRequest.cs:121-250`; `Source/Intercolony/Procurement/PurchaseOrder.cs:44-145`; `Source/Intercolony/Procurement/PurchaseOrderService.cs:100-137`). The specification must also drive UI labels, quotation pricing, post-load validity, fulfilment generation, and a per-pawn sell matcher. Adding saved fields requires a migration under the world component's explicit schema policy, whose current version is 24 (`Source/Intercolony/Core/IntercolonyWorldComponent.cs:25-30`; `Source/Intercolony/Core/IntercolonyWorldComponent.cs:1237-1485`).

The mechanical split identified by D1 also remains. Current caravan validation enumerates `AllInventoryItems`, colony validation enumerates `ThingsOfDef`, and procurement fulfilment creates things with `ThingMaker.MakeThing`; none of those item paths can implement species/sex/stage/pregnancy matching or a live-pawn handoff (`Source/Intercolony/Orders/OrderValidation.cs:146-205`; `Source/Intercolony/Orders/OrderValidation.cs:350-445`; `Source/Intercolony/Procurement/PurchaseOrderService.cs:314-370`). The discriminator and specification are necessary persisted shape, but dedicated animal discovery, matching, pricing, generation, disposal-on-failure, and transfer branches are still required. Pregnancy adds a species-capability decision, while sex and pregnancy add pricing policy because vanilla supplies no differentiating price term.

### Owner-facing recommendation on pregnancy buy-side

Do not describe buy-side pregnancy as unsafe or half-initialized for vanilla live-bearing animals: the local source verifies all required hediff state, optional-parent behavior, ticking, saving, and birth (`reference/decompiled/Verse/Hediff_Pregnant.cs:22-32`; `reference/decompiled/Verse/Hediff_Pregnant.cs:78-139`; `reference/decompiled/Verse/Hediff_Pregnant.cs:157-185`; `reference/decompiled/Verse/Hediff_Pregnant.cs:228-252`). Keep pregnancy sell-side only in V1 if the goal is the smaller and more compatible release, but make that a scope decision: buy-side becomes defensible once Intercolony restricts the option to verified live-bearing races, deterministically adds the vanilla animal hediff, post-verifies it, and defines its own pregnancy price because vanilla assigns no premium (`reference/decompiled/RimWorld/PawnUtility.cs:360-374`; `reference/decompiled/Verse/PawnCapacityUtility.cs:89-123`; `reference/decompiled/RimWorld/PriceUtility.cs:67-87`).
